## How does `spotify_connect` make an existing MA player (Sendspin/Pulsar) appear in the official Spotify app?

### Takeaway
The plugin is a **plugin provider** (`type: plugin`, `ProviderFeature.AUDIO_SOURCE`) that runs one Spotify Connect daemon *per selected MA player*. Each daemon advertises itself over mDNS as a real Connect speaker; when the Spotify app selects it, Spotify pushes audio into the daemon, and MA simultaneously starts a normal `play_media` of a synthetic `AudioSource` on the mapped MA player. The MA player is therefore the *output device behind* the Connect endpoint, not itself a Spotify Connect device.

### Cited Findings
- The plugin's own README states: "makes any Music Assistant player (or sync group) appear as a device in the official Spotify app via Spotify's Connect protocol. Selecting that device in the Spotify app streams audio into Music Assistant; the player's transport and volume controls are proxied back to Spotify." — [MA spotify_connect README](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/README.md)
- Manifest: `"type": "plugin"`, `"domain": "spotify_connect"`, `"multi_instance": false`, no Python requirements, documentation URL `https://music-assistant.io/plugins/spotify-connect/`. — [manifest.json](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/manifest.json)
- `SUPPORTED_FEATURES = {ProviderFeature.AUDIO_SOURCE}`; the provider is a `PluginProvider`. — [provider.py L98, L184](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L98)
- Per selected player: `_PlayerDaemon(player_id, safe_player_id, publish_name, stream_metadata)` plus a backend and an `AudioSource`; `identity_key = f"{self.instance_id}_{daemon.safe_player_id}"`, where `safe_player_id = re.sub(r"[^A-Za-z0-9_.-]", "_", player_id)`. Data dirs are therefore per player (`<storage>/spotify_connect/<identity_key>/...`). — [provider.py L673-696](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L673); [soloist/backend.py L183](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/backend.py#L183)
- The AudioSource is a real MA media item: `item_id=player_id`, `exclusive=True`, `allow_external_trigger=True`, `can_initiate=True`, `can_play_pause/can_seek/can_next_previous=True`; `can_shuffle`/`can_repeat` only when `backend.supports_queue_control` (Soloist only). — [provider.py L817-854](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L817)
- Audio path: backend returns `BackendStreamSource`; provider builds `StreamDetails` with the backend's display `audio_format`, the real `decoded_audio_format`, and `expiration=0` (never cache: the active-device check must re-run on every play). — [provider.py L322-363](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L322)
- Soloist audio path: daemon → private PulseAudio `module-pipe-sink` → FIFO (s32le/44.1 kHz/2ch) → MA streams controller reads it as `StreamType.NAMED_PIPE`, ffmpeg `-readrate 1 -readrate_initial_burst 0.5`. — [soloist/backend.py L336-358](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/backend.py#L336); [soloist/README.md](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md)
- go-librespot audio path: daemon decodes to `s16le` PCM on stdout (`audio_backend: pipe`, `audio_output_pipe: /dev/stdout`), delivered as `StreamType.CUSTOM` through `get_audio_stream`. — [go_librespot/backend.py L263-289](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/backend.py#L263); [go_librespot/README.md](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/README.md)
- Externally triggered playback: a `PLAYING` event schedules a **debounced (0.5 s)** `mass.player_queues.play_media(target, str(audio_source.uri))`; target is an explicitly selected player else `daemon.player_id`. — [provider.py L894-966](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L894)
- MA-side teardown releases the Spotify session: `on_source_unselected` calls `backend.deactivate()` when the queue stops clearing the source, "so the Spotify app drops the device as its playback target instead of staying tethered to it". — [provider.py L511-537](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L511)
- Deselect in Spotify app → `SESSION_INACTIVE` → `_clear_active_player` + bounded `cmd_stop` of the MA player; the active player is stopped, not just paused. — [provider.py L1044-1053, L917-934](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L1044)
- Daemons follow player lifecycle: `PLAYER_ADDED/REMOVED/CONFIG_UPDATED/UPDATED` filtered by assigned player IDs; a *temporarily unavailable* player keeps its daemon (stable advertised identity), permanent removal stops it; rename restarts the daemon because the advertised name is embedded at daemon start. — [provider.py L280-296, L615-671](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L615)
- `reload_on_streams_network_change = True` — changing the streams bind interface reloads the provider (and thus the daemons). — [provider.py L187](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L187)
- Docker image bundles the engines: `GO_LIBRESPOT_VERSION=0.9.0` downloaded into `/usr/local/bin`, plus `pulseaudio` and `pulseaudio-utils` explicitly "for MA's private audio-capture server (spotify connect soloist)". — [Dockerfile.base L237-302](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/Dockerfile.base#L245)

### Inferences
- Architecturally this is a **protocol bridge**, not a Spotify client library inside the player: Pulsar never speaks Spotify Connect, mDNS, or the Spotify protocol. It only receives an ordinary MA stream (Sendspin in the lab), exactly like any other MA queue item. Therefore no Pulsar-side work is required beyond being a normal MA player.
- Because the AudioSource is `exclusive=True`, selecting the Connect device on a player displaces whatever that player was playing; this is the mechanism behind "Spotify takes over the speaker".
- Since the plugin starts and stops daemons from player lifecycle events, a Pulsar that is offline at MA boot simply gets no advertised device until it re-registers; the Spotify device list entry disappears/appears accordingly (advertisement is owned by the per-player daemon).

### Gaps
- No upstream statement was found on the maximum number of connected players/daemons; code has no explicit cap, but each daemon costs a process (plus a Pulse sink for Soloist), so host resources are the practical limit.
- Whether MA's `AudioSource` can be started on a player that is currently in a sync group *as a member* (rather than as the group target) was not traced through the queue/streams controller.

## What exactly is advertised (device name, zeroconf/mDNS, account registration), and what are the network requirements?

### Takeaway
Each daemon advertises a **Spotify Connect mDNS service** (`_spotify-connect._tcp` in `local.`) with TXT `CPath=/`, `VERSION=1.0`, `Stack=SP`, a stable 40-hex device id, device type `speaker`, and the configured display name. There is **no account registration in MA**: the device is paired when a Spotify app on the same LAN selects it, and that app's account becomes the session account. Network requirements are flat L2 + working multicast DNS between phone and MA host, plus outbound Spotify access from the MA host.

### Cited Findings
- go-librespot registration: `z.registrar.Register(deviceName, "_spotify-connect._tcp", "local.", listenPort, []string{"CPath=/", "VERSION=1.0", "Stack=SP"})`. — [go-librespot zeroconf/zeroconf.go L92-93](https://github.com/devgianlu/go-librespot/blob/master/zeroconf/zeroconf.go)
- MA writes this go-librespot config per daemon: `device_name=<publish_name>`, `device_type="speaker"`, `device_id=sha256(identity_key)[:40]`, `zeroconf_enabled=true`, `credentials={"type":"zeroconf","zeroconf":{"persist_credentials":true}}`, API server on `127.0.0.1:<random>`. — [go_librespot/backend.py L250-305](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/backend.py#L250); [helpers.py `generate_device_id`](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/helpers.py)
- Interface selection: go-librespot is told `zeroconf_interfaces_to_advertise = [iface_of_streams_bind_ip]` when the streams bind IP maps to an interface, "so it lands on the right network on multi-homed hosts"; otherwise it advertises on all interfaces. — [go_librespot/backend.py L290-301](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/backend.py#L290)
- go-librespot default `zeroconf_port: 0` ("0 for random"); MA does not set a fixed port, so the advertised TCP port is random per daemon. — [go-librespot README, Zeroconf section](https://github.com/devgianlu/go-librespot#zeroconf-mode-and-mdns-backend-selection)
- Soloist is spawned with the name on argv: `soloist --device-name <publish_name> --api-key … --data-dir … --cache-dir … --cache-size … --initial-volume 100 --ws 127.0.0.1:0`. The device name is therefore fixed at process start (rename ⇒ restart). — [soloist/backend.py L557-586](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/backend.py#L557)
- Advertised-name template: default `"{player} | Music Assistant"`; alternatives `"{player}"` and `"Music Assistant | {player}"` (`PUBLISH_NAME_PLAYER_MASS` default, resolved by `resolve_publish_name`). — [helpers/config_entries.py L18-116](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/helpers/config_entries.py#L18); docs concur: "Player name | Music Assistant (the default), Player name only, or Music Assistant | Player name". — [MA Spotify Connect docs](https://music-assistant.io/plugins/spotify-connect/)
- Account pairing (Soloist, official Spotify doc): the API key is separate from the session; "Spotify Soloist does not open a browser or ask for a username and password. It logs in through Spotify Connect: … select the Spotify Soloist device from the device picker. Spotify Soloist authenticates and stores the session in its data directory." — [Spotify Soloist Authentication](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/soloist/concepts/authentication)
- MA's Soloist backend reinforces that it never logs in: "An `auth_state` reporting `logged_in: false` is always a `SESSION_INACTIVE`, never an error: the daemon keeps advertising itself for Connect while logged out … **MA never signs the daemon in — any Spotify account may claim it from its app.**" — [soloist/README.md L104-107](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md)
- Credentials/session persistence: Soloist data dir is `chmod 0700` because it "persists the Spotify device identity and login session"; go-librespot's config dir "doubles as the credential/device cache so the Spotify Connect device stays paired across restarts". — [soloist/backend.py L279-285](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/backend.py#L279); [go_librespot/backend.py L256-262](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/backend.py#L256)
- Network docs: "To use Spotify Connect, all devices must be connected to the same network"; "In complex network setups, if playback problems are experienced, the BIND TO option in the Streams Settings may need to be set." — [MA Spotify Connect docs](https://music-assistant.io/plugins/spotify-connect/)
- Spotify: "When connecting to the speaker for the first time, all devices need to be on the same WiFi"; pausing >10 min may require reconnecting; iPhone needs Local Network permission for Spotify. — [Spotify Connect support article](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/spotify-connect/)
- Outbound Spotify endpoints (go-librespot): access points are offered on **4070, 443 and 80**, with 4070 first; `prefer_firewall_friendly_ports: true` tries 443 → 80 → 4070. "The dealer and spclient … already use 443." — [go-librespot README, Additional configuration](https://github.com/devgianlu/go-librespot#additional-configuration)
- Lab deployment already runs MA with `network_mode: host` and the compose comment "MA must be on the host network for mDNS/uPnP discovery and streaming". — local: `projects/home-assistant/stack/docker-compose.yml`
- Lab host is multi-homed on one /24: `enp3s0 192.0.2.26`, `wlp4s0 192.0.2.25`, both static. — local: `projects/home-assistant/INVENTORY.md`

### Inferences
- Required inbound path: UDP 5353 multicast (mDNS) from phone/desktop to the MA host, plus the daemon's random TCP port (and whatever Spotify's access point connection needs). Because the port is random for go-librespot and the service is mDNS-discovered, **Docker bridge networking with published ports will not work**; host networking (or macvlan) is effectively mandatory, consistent with MA's compose comment.
- On the lab's dual-NIC host, a daemon advertising on both `192.0.2.26` and `192.0.2.25` may appear once but resolve to the "wrong" NIC, or produce confusing duplicates. Setting Streams → BIND TO to the wired `192.0.2.26` makes go-librespot advertise only on `enp3s0` (code maps bind IP → interface). For Soloist, no interface filter is visible in MA code, so it likely advertises on all interfaces — an untested risk on this host.
- Soloist's mDNS service/TXT/port are produced by Spotify's own binary and were not verified byte-for-byte here; the `_spotify-connect._tcp` + `CPath=/`, `VERSION=1.0`, `Stack=SP` shape is the protocol's, demonstrated by the go-librespot implementation and by Spotify's documented pairing flow. Treat "Soloist uses exactly the same TXT set" as informed inference, not measured.
- Because pairing is per-daemon and stored in its data dir, deleting MA's `spotify_connect` storage (or the container's `/data`) loses the pairing and requires re-selecting the device in the Spotify app — but never re-entering a Spotify password.

### Gaps
- No primary source enumerates the *exact* inbound/outbound firewall matrix for Spotify Connect (only the access-point port hints above). The random advertised port means a strict firewall cannot be configured from documentation alone; measurement (pcap) would be needed.
- Whether Soloist honours an interface-selection hint from MA (it is not passed on argv) is unknown from the 2.10.8 code.

## Which playback engines are supported (`soloist`, `go_librespot`), and how do they differ?

### Takeaway
Two interchangeable engines behind one backend contract. **Soloist** is Spotify's official headless client: API key + managed download, any account type (Free-with-ads or Premium), lossless up to 24-bit/44.1 kHz, queue verbs, two volume policies, Linux-only, 90-day build expiry, ToS grey area. **go-librespot** is a community reverse-engineered client: zero setup, Premium-family accounts created before December 2024 only, Ogg Vorbis ≤320 kbps, transport-only, stdout PCM. New setups default to Soloist; configs predating the backend split migrate to go-librespot.

### Cited Findings
- Engine choice is setup data (`CONF_BACKEND`), values `soloist` / `go_librespot`; a config with no backend value yields `None`, which "intentionally selects go-librespot". — [provider.py L74-82, L741-782](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L741); [setup_flow.py](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/setup_flow.py)
- Setup flow default and labels: Soloist = "official, recommended", "Setup takes a few extra steps and requires a Premium account to create a personal API key"; go-librespot = "Needs no API key or download, but is intended for accounts created before December 2024 and may stop working when Spotify changes its service." — [strings.json](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/strings.json)
- Comparison table in the provider README: origin official vs reverse-engineered; setup key vs none; accounts "Free (with ads) or Premium" vs "Premium-family accounts created before Dec 2024"; max quality "Lossless up to 24-bit/44.1 kHz" vs "Ogg Vorbis 320 kbps"; audio delivery Pulse pipe-sink/FIFO vs stdout pipe; pause behavior provider-stops-player vs clean EOF; queue control yes vs transport only; volume "pin at 100% or sync with compensation" vs `external_volume`; risk "binary from Spotify CDN, 90-day expiry, ToS grey area" vs "may break when Spotify changes protocol". — [spotify_connect README L66-79](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/README.md)
- Soloist platform gate: `_resolve_architecture()` raises `UnsupportedPlatformError` if `platform.system() != "Linux"` — "soloist is only available for Linux". The setup flow then forces `go_librespot` with error `soloist_unsupported_platform`. — [soloist/runtime.py L992-1017](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/runtime.py#L992); [setup_flow.py L88-115](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/setup_flow.py#L88)
- Soloist binary management: downloaded from Spotify's CDN only after explicit consent, validated (tar structure, ELF, arch), installed atomically under MA storage, shared by all daemons; builds expire 90 days after build date and the daemon then exits with code 10; three refresh layers + 60 s verify cache. — [soloist/README.md L35-56](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md)
- Soloist capture: "Soloist has no pipe/stdout output of its own — it plays into an audio server"; MA runs a private PulseAudio daemon with one `module-pipe-sink` per Connect daemon; failed sink volume ops "fail closed" (tear down and rebuild). — [soloist/README.md L58-77](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md)
- Soloist `stream_ends_on_pause = False` (pipe sink never EOFs), so MA actively stops the player on `PAUSED`; `supports_queue_control = True` (add-to-queue, shuffle, repeat, queue events). — [soloist/backend.py L257-266](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/backend.py#L257)
- go-librespot binary: resolved from `PATH`; the Docker image/HA add-on install it at build time (0.9.0 in 2.10.8); manual installs need `brew install go-librespot` or a release binary. Provider load fails if missing. — [helpers.py](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/helpers.py); [go_librespot/README.md L33-48](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/README.md)
- go-librespot known limitations in MA: cold start needs a prior Spotify context (otherwise a localized error sends the user to the app); sync-group elapsed time can drift because position is the daemon's decode point. — [go_librespot/README.md L95-101](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/README.md)
- Quality mapping: central tiers `normal=96`, `high=160`, `very_high=320`, `lossless`; `LOSSY_BIT_RATES[lossless]=320` for engines that cannot do lossless; go-librespot `bitrate` "only accepts 96, 160 and 320", so lossless is capped; Soloist writes `audio.play_bitrate_enumeration` 2/3/4/5 where 5 = lossless FLAC (~810 kbps measured). — [base.py L20-82](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/base.py#L20); [soloist/README.md L124-147](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md)
- Normalization/crossfade are applied *by the engine*: go-librespot `normalisation_disabled` / `crossfade_duration` (ms, needs >=0.8.0); Soloist rewrites classic desktop prefs `audio.crossfade_v2`, `audio.crossfade.time_v2`, `audio.normalize_v2` before every spawn. — [go_librespot/backend.py L280-287](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/backend.py#L280); [soloist/README.md L109-122](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md)
- Official Spotify Soloist availability: "Run Spotify playback on Linux, Apple Silicon Mac, and x86_64 Windows devices"; API key required at startup; builds expire after 90 days; the key-generating account must have Premium. (MA's own gate is stricter: Linux only.) — [Spotify Soloist docs](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/soloist); [Authentication](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/soloist/concepts/authentication)
- Lab already runs Soloist for the *music* provider: "Spotify Soloist `1.3.10.13`, web player, playback проверен". — local: `docs/STATE.md`

### Inferences
- On the lab's Linux x86_64 Docker host, Soloist is the only engine that can deliver lossless and is the only one that supports Spotify accounts created after Dec 2024 — decisive if the household account is new. The existing working Soloist install also means the binary/CDN/ToS path is already proven on this host.
- Soloist's PulseAudio dependency is satisfied inside the container (image ships `pulseaudio`), so no host PulseAudio/desktop session is required; MA starts its own private daemon.
- go-librespot is the fallback when the API-key step is unacceptable or impossible, not a quality-equivalent alternative.

### Gaps
- Actual delivered codec/bitrate is unobservable by design (Soloist keeps no quality field, encrypted cache, log level not exposed); MA reports the configured ceiling. — [soloist/README.md L134-147](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md)
- No measured data on CPU/RAM cost per Soloist daemon (one process + one Pulse sink each) on this host.

## Does the provider need Spotify Web API credentials or a Spotify music provider? Does it use the same single-stream account slot?

### Takeaway
**No Web API credentials and no Spotify music provider are required.** The only credential is an optional Soloist API key (for the engine binary), which is not a Web API token. The Connect device is claimed by whichever Spotify account selects it in the app; while that account is streaming to it, it occupies that account's **single active stream**, i.e. the same slot MA's Spotify music provider uses when it is signed into the same account. There is no coordination between the two providers in 2.10.8.

### Cited Findings
- "Music Assistant needs **no** Spotify Web API credentials and **no** configured Spotify *music* provider for this." — [spotify_connect README L6-7](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/README.md)
- go-librespot backend docstring: "Playback control works without a configured Spotify *music* provider or the Spotify Web API." — [go_librespot/backend.py L1-7](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/backend.py#L1)
- Soloist API key is a Spotify-for-Developers *Soloist* key, generated at `/dashboard/soloist`, stored as `ConfigEntryType.SECURE_STRING`, passed only on the daemon argv and redacted from logs. It is not a client id/secret and grants no Web API access. — [setup_flow.py L136-183](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/setup_flow.py#L136); [strings.json `soloist_api_key`](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/strings.json); [Spotify Soloist Authentication](https://developer.spotify.com/documentation/soloist/concepts/authentication)
- MA's Spotify *music* provider documents the account limit: "One stream slot: a Spotify account supports a single active stream, and the run is it." — [spotify/README.md, Soloist specifics](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify/README.md)
- Spotify's consumer docs/community state one active stream per account; starting on a second device stops the first ("You can only stream music to one device at a time"). — [Spotify Community KB](https://community.spotify.com/forum/en/kb/articles/834-premium-family-duo-only-one-person-can-listen-at-a-time); [Spotify Connect support](https://support.spotify.com/us/article/spotify-connect/) (same-network requirement; no multi-stream promise)
- The Connect device is account-agnostic: "MA never signs the daemon in — any Spotify account may claim it from its app." — [soloist/README.md L104-107](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md)
- The plugin has no cross-references to the Spotify music provider beyond importing the shared Soloist infrastructure and quality tiers: `providers/spotify/backends/soloist.py`, `spotify/setup_flow.py`, `spotify/helpers.py` import `spotify_connect.soloist` / `spotify_connect.base`; no stream-limit, queue, or account guard exists in `spotify_connect`. — repo-wide grep at 2.10.8; [spotify/backends/soloist.py](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify/backends/soloist.py); [spotify_connect/provider.py](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py)
- MA's Spotify music provider in Soloist single-track mode "advertises no Connect device and disables remote control and transfer for its playback context"; conversely, "playing on the account from another device can still kill the stream Spotify-side". — [spotify/README.md](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify/README.md)

### Inferences
- **Same account:** Connect playback and Spotify-music-provider playback cannot run simultaneously. Starting MA queue playback of a Spotify track while the Connect device is playing will transfer/stop playback at Spotify, and MA will observe the Connect session ending (`SESSION_INACTIVE` → stop player), or the music-provider item will fail; which side visibly "wins" depends on timing. Starting Spotify playback on the phone while MA is streaming a Spotify track to Pulsar should likewise end the music-provider run (documented in the music-provider README for that direction) and take over the Connect device.
- **Different accounts:** no slot conflict — a household member's account can claim the Connect device while MA's music provider streams on the key owner's account, because Spotify's limit is per account, not per server. This is a genuine advantage of the Connect plugin's account-agnostic pairing.
- Because the plugin bypasses the Web API, a Web API rate-limit (the lab saw HTTP 429 with ~18.5 h Retry-After) should **not** block Connect playback or device discovery; only catalog/library features of the music provider remain broken. This makes Connect a plausible workaround for the lab's current 429, but it cannot replace library browsing/voice "play <track>" via Spotify.
- The Soloist API key owner only needs Premium at key-generation time; the listening account may be Free-with-ads. Billing/ToS risk therefore sits with the key owner, not necessarily the listener.

### Gaps
- No primary code or doc states what MA does if the Connect device and the music provider are used with the same account *at the same instant*; behavior is Spotify-side and was not measured here.
- Whether Spotify's single-stream rule treats a *paused* Connect session as occupying the slot (vs only active playback) was not established; the practical rule is "one active playback at a time".

## What metadata/transport/volume state flows, and how do phone→speaker transfer and speaker→phone takeover work?

### Takeaway
Audio and metadata flow **Spotify → engine → MA**; commands and volume flow **MA → engine → Spotify**. MA surfaces the playing track as live `StreamMetadata` on the synthetic AudioSource queue item (title/artist/album/artwork/duration/position), proxies play/pause/next/previous/seek/shuffle/repeat, and keeps volume in sync with an explicit policy. Phone→speaker transfer is a Spotify-side session activation that makes MA start `play_media`; speaker→phone takeover produces `SESSION_INACTIVE` and MA stops the player. Pressing play in MA after a takeover re-activates the device by replaying the last context.

### Cited Findings
- Metadata model: `BackendTrackMetadata(track_uri, title, artist, album, image_url, duration, position)`; provider writes it into `daemon.stream_metadata` and pushes to the active queue item via `mass.players.update_source_metadata(...)` — "the same channel ICY radio metadata uses". — [provider.py L1088-1104, L1160-1171](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L1088); [models.py](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/models.py)
- Before any metadata arrives the item title is `f"Spotify Connect | {publish_name}"`; `POSITION` events update `elapsed_time` + `elapsed_time_last_updated`. — [provider.py L681-686, L1090-1092](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L1090)
- Transport proxy: `SourceControl.PLAY → resume()`, `PAUSE → pause()`, `NEXT → next()`, `PREVIOUS → previous()`, `SEEK → seek(int(value)*1000)`, `SHUFFLE → set_shuffle(bool)`, `REPEAT → set_repeat(RepeatMode)`; commands are refused with a localized "not the active Spotify playback device" error when neither playing nor session-active. — [provider.py L559-596](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L559)
- Shuffle/repeat are mirrored back into the MA queue (`update_source_options`) for queue-capable backends, including a cached pre-claim value. — [provider.py L469-478, L1137-1158](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L1137)
- Queue snapshots are explicitly **not** consumed yet: `if event.type is QUEUE_CHANGED: return` ("full queue-item mirroring comes later"). — [provider.py L1020-1022](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L1020)
- Volume policy (Soloist): `player_only` (default) pins the daemon at 100% so unity-gain PCM reaches the FIFO, MA owns audible volume, and the Spotify slider stays at 100%; `sync_spotify` attenuates in the daemon with Spotify's cubic curve and compensates in the sink with `sink_pct = 10000 / spotify_pct` (exact inverse of PulseAudio's cubic law); a large upward jump can cause a brief full-scale artifact. — [soloist/README.md L79-92](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md); [strings.json `volume_mode`](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/strings.json)
- Volume policy (go-librespot): `external_volume: true`, `volume_steps: 100`, daemon starts at 100% and never attenuates PCM; volume events still flow both ways. — [go_librespot/backend.py L57-60, L276-290](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/backend.py#L276)
- Volume sync loop guards: provider pushes the player's volume on `SESSION_ACTIVE` and on source claim; Spotify-side volume events are ignored for `INITIAL_VOLUME_GRACE_S = 3.0` s after activation (player's volume wins on reconnect) and de-duplicated against `last_volume_sent` to prevent ping-pong; for a group, `group_volume` is pushed because a group has no level of its own. — [provider.py L116-118, L1173-1249](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L1173)
- Phone→speaker: `SESSION_ACTIVE` sets `spotify_session_active`, syncs volume, and `_schedule_play_media`; `PLAYING` sets `playing=True` and schedules the debounced `play_media` (0.5 s) so a dying session's stale `playing` cannot start a stream. — [provider.py L1028-1066, L941-966](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L1028)
- Speaker→phone / deselect: `SESSION_INACTIVE` clears the active player and schedules a stop of the MA player; the stop is replaced/cancelled if a `PLAYING` arrives while it is in flight. — [provider.py L1044-1059](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L1044)
- Take-back from MA: if the device is still session-active but paused, `resume()`; if the user moved playback away (`last_context_uri` known), `backend.play(last_context_uri, skip_to_uri=last_track_uri)`, which "makes this device the active one again"; Soloist's `play()` calls `activate` first because "a bare play would start local playback without a Connect transfer, leaving the Spotify apps unaware." — [provider.py L480-505](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L480); [soloist/README.md L94-107](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md)
- Take-back caveat: "The track restarts from its beginning (there is no resume-at-position play call)"; Soloist additionally does not support `skip_to_uri` — `"skip_to_uri is not supported by soloist; starting %s from the beginning"`. — [provider.py L487-496](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L487); [soloist/backend.py L365-375](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/backend.py#L365)
- Pause behavior differs: Soloist pipe sink never EOFs, so MA stops the player on pause (`stream_ends_on_pause=False`); go-librespot's stdout stream ends cleanly (provider reports `stream_ends_on_pause=True`), so the player leaves playing state and the next `playing` re-streams. — [base.py L107-115](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/base.py#L107); [go_librespot/README.md L55-64](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/go_librespot/README.md)
- Known UX caveats: buffering can delay app actions audibly; "the Spotify app's progress can run slightly ahead of the audio"; delivered encoding/quality is invisible, MA shows decoded PCM (44.1 kHz/32-bit with Soloist). — [MA Spotify Connect docs](https://music-assistant.io/plugins/spotify-connect/)

### Inferences
- MA sends **no track metadata to Spotify**: Spotify is authoritative for catalog/queue state, and the engine reports state to MA. What MA "sends back" is limited to transport verbs, seek position, shuffle/repeat, volume, and activation/deactivation of the device.
- Because `QUEUE_CHANGED` is ignored, the MA queue cannot show the upcoming Spotify tracks; the UI shows a single live item. "Next" therefore must be issued as a source-control command (UI, API, or voice mapped to source control), not by advancing an MA queue.
- The 3 s volume grace plus the "player's volume wins" rule means the Spotify slider will visibly jump to the speaker's current level right after transfer; with Soloist `player_only` it will then stay pinned at 100% while the real level is changed on Pulsar/MA.
- The forced restart-from-beginning on take-back is a real product limitation for audiobooks/podcasts: a "continue where I left off" flow only works phone→speaker (Spotify resumes its own position), not MA→Spotify after a takeover.

### Gaps
- No measurement of end-to-end latency from Spotify app command to Pulsar audio; the docs only say "short delay depending on the player's own buffering".
- Whether Pulsar/Sendspin reports position back to MA in a way that could correct the "app runs ahead" drift was not investigated.
- Soloist "sync_spotify" volume behavior on a Sendspin player (which may apply its own DSP/volume curve) is untested; the cubic compensation proof is for the Pulse sink, not for the player's own volume stage.

## Can it coexist with MA's normal Spotify **music** provider in the same instance? What conflicts arise?

### Takeaway
Yes — they are independent provider domains (`spotify` music provider vs `spotify_connect` plugin) and can be installed side by side; they even share the Soloist binary/runtime. They do **not** share queues, sources, or credentials. The real conflicts are (a) the per-account single active stream if both use the same Spotify account, (b) player takeover because the Connect AudioSource is exclusive, and (c) control/UX fragmentation: the Connect item is one opaque live source with no queue mirroring and no catalog search, while the music provider owns catalog/voice search.

### Cited Findings
- Separate domains and provider types: `spotify` is a music provider with Web API + playback backends; `spotify_connect` is a `plugin` with `AUDIO_SOURCE` only. — [spotify/manifest.json](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify/manifest.json); [spotify_connect/manifest.json](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/manifest.json)
- Shared infrastructure, not shared state: the music provider's Soloist backend imports `spotify_connect.soloist.runtime` and `spotify_connect.base`; the Connect README notes "the Spotify music provider's own soloist playback backend reuses it rather than shipping a second copy." — [spotify/backends/soloist.py L52-62](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify/backends/soloist.py#L52); [soloist/README.md L30-33](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/README.md)
- Distinct data dirs: music provider keeps its own `<storage>/spotify/<instance_id>/soloist-data`; Connect keeps `<storage>/spotify_connect/<identity_key>/soloist-data` per player. — [spotify/README.md](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify/README.md); [spotify_connect/soloist/backend.py L183](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/soloist/backend.py#L183)
- Music-provider playback is "an ordinary single-stream source"; its capacity error is `ProviderStreamLimitError` when a second item is requested. The Connect plugin is not part of that capacity machinery (it is an AudioSource, not a music provider). — [spotify/README.md](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify/README.md); repo grep shows no `StreamLimit`/capacity references in `spotify_connect`
- Player-level exclusivity: the Connect AudioSource is `exclusive=True`, and `on_source_selected` explicitly stops a different player that was consuming it. Conversely, any other queue playback on the same player replaces the source, which triggers `on_source_unselected → deactivate()`. — [provider.py L426-467, L511-537](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/provider.py#L426)
- MA classifies `spotify_connect` as an external source and gives a paused external source a 60 s grace before considering the session ended (`EXTERNAL_PAUSE_IDLE_TIMEOUT = 60`). — [constants.py L1055-1070](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/constants.py#L1055)
- Setup flow migration: pre-backend-split Connect configs become **go-librespot** (not Soloist); a fresh setup preselects Soloist. The plugin was collapsed from per-player instances into one single-instance provider driven by the connected-players multi-select. — [setup_flow.py](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/providers/spotify_connect/setup_flow.py); [migrations.py L815-990](https://github.com/music-assistant/server/blob/2.10.8/music_assistant/controllers/config/migrations.py#L815)
- Lab currently has the Spotify **music** provider configured with Soloist and a signed-in account (Liked Songs playlist exists but track sync is blocked by HTTP 429). — local: `docs/STATE.md`

### Inferences
- **Recommended architecture for this lab:** keep the Spotify music provider for catalog/library/voice-driven playback, add the Connect plugin for "phone → speaker" Spotify sessions. If both use the *same* Spotify account, treat them as mutually exclusive at playback time; do not expect MA to arbitrate. If the household can claim the Connect device with a *different* account, the two can genuinely play different streams simultaneously.
- Starting a Spotify album from MA (music provider) while the Connect device is streaming on the same player is the worst case: it both changes the player's source (exclusive) and takes the account's stream, so the Connect session is torn down twice over. Expect a stop/reconnect cycle rather than a clean handoff.
- Voice/assistant behavior follows from the source model: transport words ("pause", "next", "громче") map to Connect source controls and work; "play <track/album>" through MA needs a music provider (Spotify, Yandex Music, etc.) and will replace the Connect source, ending the Spotify session on that player. Catalog search is impossible from the Connect plugin alone.
- The 60 s external-pause timeout means a Spotify pause longer than a minute stops presenting the source as resumable; resuming from the phone after that re-triggers activation/`play_media` rather than continuing the same MA stream — consistent with Spotify's own ">10 min may need reconnect" note, but stricter on the MA side.
- Two Soloist consumers (music provider single-track runs + Connect daemons) can coexist on disk and in process space because each has its own data dir and the binary manager serializes installs; the constraint is the account/stream, not the engine.

### Gaps
- No test evidence of the exact interleaving when the same account is used by both providers; no upstream issue/doc describing a supported "handoff" workflow.
- Whether MA's `EXTERNAL_PAUSE_IDLE_TIMEOUT` path and the Connect provider's own pause-stop path interact badly for a paused Spotify session was not traced end-to-end.
- Voice intent mapping to `SourceControl` (vs queue commands) depends on the conversation agent/integration version; not verified for the lab's HA Assist pipeline.

## What is the setup flow in MA 2.10.8, and what configuration is needed for a Pulsar player?

### Takeaway
Install **Settings → Plugins → Add a plugin → Spotify Connect** (once), choose Soloist (default, recommended) or go-librespot, complete Soloist consent + API key if chosen, then in the plugin settings select the Pulsar player under **Connected players**, optionally pick the advertised-name template, volume mode (Soloist only), crossfade, normalization and quality. No Spotify sign-in, no music provider, no player-side changes. On the lab host the prerequisites are already met (MA 2.10.8 on host network, Pulsar registered as a Sendspin player, Soloist working).

### Cited Findings
- Documented flow: "Settings → Plugins → click Add a plugin → select `Spotify Connect`. The plugin only needs to be added once"; choose engine; Soloist setup walks through terms and API key; then "open the plugin's settings. Under **Connected players**, select every player that should appear in the Spotify app. Each selected player is advertised as its own Spotify Connect device"; optionally change **Advertised device name**; "Renaming a player renames its device." — [MA Spotify Connect docs](https://music-assistant.io/plugins/spotify-connect/)

- First-use step: "Open the Spotify app… select the Music Assistant player by its name. The audio will now play through that player." — [MA Spotify Connect docs](https://music-assistant.io/plugins/spotify-connect/)
- Pulsar is a native Music Assistant **Sendspin** player in addition to an ESPHome/HA device; it plays HTTP streams through the firmware's GStreamer and controls volume via ALSA. — local: `projects/station/upstream/pulsar/README.md`
- Lab: "MA автоматически увидела Sendspin-плеер «Яндекс Станция Миди»"; MA `2.10.8`, host network, `:8095` UI, `:8097` stream, `:8927` Sendspin. — local: `docs/HISTORY.md`, `projects/home-assistant/INVENTORY.md`
- MA docs warn: "Home Assistant players are supported but not recommended — your mileage may vary. Native Music Assistant players work best." — [MA Spotify Connect docs](https://music-assistant.io/plugins/spotify-connect/)
- Release context: 2.10 added the **Yandex Station** player provider (local control of Yandex speakers) alongside the existing Sendspin path. — [MA 2.10 release notes](https://www.music-assistant.io/blog/2026/08/26/music-assistant-2-10/)

### Proposed configuration for this lab (inference, not upstream doc)
1. **Prerequisites check (read-only):** MA container still `network_mode: host`; Streams → BIND TO set to `192.0.2.26` on `enp3s0` (multi-homed host; avoids advertising on Wi-Fi and keeps the player-facing stream on the wired LAN); Pulsar player online in MA as a Sendspin player named «Яндекс Станция Миди»; MA host can reach Spotify (already proven by Soloist playback; egress guard must not block 443/4070).
2. **Install plugin** once: Settings → Plugins → Add plugin → *Spotify Connect* (beta). Do **not** add it per player; one instance covers all.
3. **Engine:** choose **Spotify Soloist** (official, Linux host, lossless, supports post-Dec-2024 accounts). Accept the download/ToS consent, paste the Soloist API key generated at `developer.spotify.com/dashboard/soloist` (Premium required once; the listening account can be Free). If the key step is unacceptable, choose go-librespot — but verify the household account predates Dec 2024.
4. **Connected players:** select only the native Sendspin instance of the Pulsar (not an HA-bridged duplicate, if both are listed). One device appears per selected player/group.
5. **Advertised name:** choose *Player name only* if the goal is to look like a native Yandex speaker in Spotify (device shows as «Яндекс Станция Миди»), otherwise keep the default suffix `| Music Assistant` to distinguish it from real Spotify hardware.
6. **Volume:** `player_only` (default) if Pulsar/ALSA should own loudness and Spotify should stay at 100%; `sync_spotify` if the family will use the Spotify slider. Expect the documented brief artifact on large upward jumps in sync mode.
7. **Audio:** quality `lossless` (Soloist; Pulsar's Sendspin path resamples as needed anyway), loudness normalization on (Spotify's own −14 LUFS), crossfade 0 initially (crossfade only affects engine-queue transitions, not album/playlist contexts).
8. **Pairing:** play something in the Spotify app on the phone (same Wi-Fi/L2), open the device picker, select «Яндекс Станция Миди». First selection creates and stores the session in `<MA data>/spotify_connect/spotify_connect_<safe_player_id>/soloist-data`; no Spotify password is ever entered in MA.
9. **Verify:** phone→speaker transfer starts Pulsar via MA (metadata appears on the MA queue item); choosing the phone in Spotify stops Pulsar; pressing play on the MA source re-activates the speaker (track restarts); stopping the MA queue releases the device in Spotify.
10. **Coexistence policy:** if the music provider uses the same Spotify account, avoid concurrent playback; treat Connect as the "phone handoff" path and MA catalog playback as the "MA queue" path. If possible, claim the Connect device with a different household account to remove the single-stream contention entirely.

### Inferences
- No Pulsar firmware change is required: the plugin only needs a registered playback-capable MA player; Pulsar's Sendspin endpoint already satisfies that, and MA does the Spotify protocol work on the server.
- Selecting the HA-bridged Pulsar instead of the native Sendspin one would add an extra hop and is exactly the "HA players … not recommended" case; use the native player.
- A group containing Pulsar can be exposed as one Spotify device (docs: groups supported); volume is then taken from `group_volume`, per the provider code.
- Because the first pairing requires same-L2 mDNS, a phone on cellular/VPN or on a guest VLAN will not see the device even though the MA host is reachable by IP; Spotify's own docs confirm the same-Wi-Fi requirement.

### Gaps
- No upstream confirmation that Soloist is supported inside the official MA Docker image on this exact host (only that the image ships PulseAudio and that the lab's music-provider Soloist 1.3.10.13 works; the Connect provider's Soloist path is the same runtime but was not run here).
- No measurement of Pulsar-specific behavior under `sync_spotify` volume mode, lossless tier, or long pauses (>60 s) — all flagged as risks rather than observed.
- The exact Spotify app UI wording/labels differ by platform and locale; the flow above is functional, not a literal UI transcription.
